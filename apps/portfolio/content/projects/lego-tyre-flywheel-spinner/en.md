## The science hidden inside a spinning top

A spinning top looks simple, but many interesting physics ideas are hidden inside it. The first important idea is **angular momentum**. When a spinning top spins very fast, it tries to keep spinning in the same direction. This is why it can stay balanced instead of falling over. The faster it spins, the larger its angular momentum becomes, and the harder it is to tip over.

However, a spinning top does not spin forever. **Friction** between the tip and the floor, and **air resistance**, slowly reduce its speed. As it slows, the angular momentum becomes smaller, and eventually the top loses its balance and falls.

Another important factor is the **centre of mass**. A spinning top works best when its centre of mass is close to the spinning axis. If the centre of mass is off to one side, the top will wobble and lose balance more quickly.

## String Launcher Prototype

![String Launcher Prototype](/projects/lego-tyre-flywheel-spinner/string-launcher.jpg)

This was one of my first LEGO spinning tops. It was made from **two gears, two wheels, and one axle**. The small gear was used to wrap the string around so I could launch the top. The larger wheel was placed near the outside edge to make it more stable, and the axle connected all the parts together so the top could rotate smoothly.

Although it was a simple design, it was heavy and well balanced. Even today it is still one of my strongest spinning tops, because its weight is spread evenly around the outside.

## Multi-Gear Prototype

![Multi-Gear Prototype](/projects/lego-tyre-flywheel-spinner/multi-gear.jpg)

For my second design, I wanted the top to be easier to spin. This model used **three gears, one wheel, and one axle**. Instead of one gear to hold the string, I added three, which gave me more space to wrap the string and made it easier to launch.

But during my experiments I discovered this design was lighter than my first model. Although it was easier to launch, it did not stay balanced for as long, because the weight was not distributed as well. This taught me that **adding more parts does not always make a better design.**

## Ripcord Launcher Version

![Ripcord Launcher Version](/projects/lego-tyre-flywheel-spinner/ripcord-version.jpg)

Next, I built a completely different design from **two wheels, one gear, and one axle**. By this time I had stopped using the string, because I found a better solution — the **LEGO Ripcord Launcher**. I designed a longer axle so the launcher could connect directly to the top. This made launching much faster and much easier. Even though it had fewer pieces, it was more stable than my second design because the weight was arranged more carefully.

## Lightweight Prototype

![Lightweight Prototype](/projects/lego-tyre-flywheel-spinner/lightweight.jpg)

One of my simplest designs used **only one wheel and one axle**. It was very light and easy to build. But because it had very little weight, it could not store as much spinning energy, so it stopped spinning much sooner than my other designs. Although it was not my best performer, it helped me understand how important weight and balance are.

## LEGO Tyre Gear Prototype

![LEGO Tyre Gear Prototype](/projects/lego-tyre-flywheel-spinner/tyre-gear.jpg)

This spinning top is built from **one large LEGO tyre, one small gear, and one axle**. The large rubber tyre is the most important part: it forms the outer rim and places most of the weight far from the centre, which helps the top stay balanced and spin for a longer time. The yellow axle at the bottom acts as the spinning tip — the only part that touches the ground. With only a few pieces, this design gives excellent balance, smooth rotation, and a long spinning time.

## Single-Tyre Spinning Top

![Single-Tyre Spinning Top](/projects/lego-tyre-flywheel-spinner/single-tyre.jpg)

This top is built from **one large LEGO tyre, two gears, and one axle**. I chose a tyre because most of its weight sits around the outside edge, which helps balance and a longer spin. A large gear supports the tyre and keeps the structure strong; below it a smaller gear connects to the axle. The axle runs through the centre as the spinning axis and lets the top connect to the ripcord launcher. With only a few pieces, it performs very well.

## Launching: from string to ripcord

![LEGO Ripcord Launcher](/projects/lego-tyre-flywheel-spinner/ripcord-launcher.jpg)

Building the spinning top was only half of the challenge. The other half was finding a good way to launch it. At first I wrapped a string around a small gear. It worked, but it was hard to pull the string quickly and smoothly — sometimes it slipped, and sometimes the top did not spin fast enough to stay balanced.

To solve this, I added a longer axle so I could connect the top to a **LEGO ripcord launcher**. The ripcord can spin the top much faster than I can by hand, so the tops became much more stable and could spin for a longer time. This was one of the biggest improvements in my project.

## What I discovered

![My LEGO parts](/projects/lego-tyre-flywheel-spinner/parts.jpg)

While testing, I discovered that spinning speed is not the only thing that matters — the **arrangement of the weight** is just as important. Tops with more weight around the outside edge spun more smoothly and stayed upright longer, while tops with the weight near the centre stopped much sooner. The weight around the outside helps the top resist changes in its motion. After this discovery, I used LEGO wheels, tyres, and large gears around the edge of my designs whenever possible.
