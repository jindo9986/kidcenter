# Hi, I'm Tin.

My full name is Đào Đình Hữu.

If I had to describe myself in one sentence, I would say that I really enjoy understanding how things work.

Ever since I was little, I have been curious about the world around me.

I often find myself asking questions.

Why does it happen?

How does it work?

What was it like before?

What would happen if something changed?

Sometimes these questions come from something I see in everyday life.

Sometimes they come from a museum visit, an animal, a fossil, a science experiment, or a topic I happen to discover.

One question often leads to another, and before I know it, I am spending a lot of time trying to learn more about something that caught my attention.

Science is the subject that feels most natural to me.

I like that science begins with curiosity and encourages people to ask questions, observe carefully, look for evidence, and discover answers.

Many of the things I enjoy learning about connect to science in some way.

I enjoy learning about animals, dinosaurs, nature, technology, history, and how different things work.

The more I learn, the more connected everything seems.

One of my favorite hobbies is reading.

I enjoy reading books about science, nature, animals, history, technology, and many other topics that interest me.

Reading helps me continue exploring the questions that I am curious about.

Sometimes a book answers one question.

Sometimes it gives me three new ones.

Either way, I usually end up wanting to learn more.

Another way I learn is through observation.

I enjoy visiting museums, zoos, aquariums, science centers, and exhibitions.

I like looking closely at details and thinking about what they might tell me.

A fossil makes me wonder about life millions of years ago.

An animal makes me curious about how it survives in its environment.

A scientific model makes me want to understand how something works.

Many of my interests have grown from simply taking the time to observe and ask questions.

I also enjoy drawing.

For me, drawing is not only about creating pictures.

It is a way of learning.

When I draw an animal, a dinosaur, or something I am studying, I begin to notice details that I did not see before.

Drawing helps me slow down, observe more carefully, and understand things more deeply.

The more closely I look, the more I learn.

I enjoy mathematics as well because it challenges me to think logically and solve problems.

I like finding patterns, exploring different solutions, and figuring out how things connect.

Over the years, I have been fortunate to participate in many Mathematics, Science, and Astronomy Olympiads at school, city, national, and international levels.

I have received Gold, Silver, Bronze, and Honours awards, and I was especially proud to be recognized as a Natural Sciences Star Student for two consecutive years.

These achievements are important to me because they remind me of the learning, effort, and persistence behind them.

I enjoy the excitement of solving difficult problems, discovering new ideas, and gradually understanding things that once seemed challenging.

My teachers often describe me as curious, thoughtful, independent, and eager to learn.

When I think about the things I enjoy most, they usually follow the same path.

First, I notice something.

Then I become curious about it.

I ask questions.

I investigate.

I read.

I observe.

And little by little, I begin to understand.

If I had to describe my learning journey in three words, they would be:

**Observe. Investigate. Understand.**

There is still so much that I want to discover.

That is what makes learning exciting.

Every answer feels like the beginning of a new question, and I look forward to finding where those questions will lead me next.
